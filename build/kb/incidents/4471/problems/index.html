How the reports were split

  1. Count each report once. The export repeats some rows. One id is one report.
  2. Place each report by its tenant, not by its intake tag. The tenant’s locale and booking form decide the cause (causeOf). Intake guesses the tag from the reporter’s browser, and the guess is often wrong.
  3. Take out the one-day moves the desk noted (desk-notes.json). In these reports the date moves by one day. Day and month do not swap.

The parser reads dates month first

The shared parser reads every date month first. Our tenants write the day first, so day and month swap: a booking for 7 June goes out for 6 July. The queued fix for #4471 is for this problem. Every day-first tenant has it, through every channel.

The booking-v3 hint says MM/DD/YYYY

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants on booking-v3 type the month first, as the hint tells them. The form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first, and the date swaps. This happens only through the form. A fix to the parser does not change it: the hint must show the tenant’s own date order.

Invites move by one day

These reports have no swap. The date is one day early or late:

  • “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting. We held it twice.”
  • “The invite arrived before I made the booking.”
  • “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday.”

People in different places see different days for the same booking, so look at time zone handling, not at date order. A fix to the parser does not change it.

The three problems share subject lines such as “Wrong day on the invite”. The desk merges on subject alone (decision 0001), so all three went into one ticket. Before you merge a date report, check its tenant, its channel, and whether the day moved by one.
